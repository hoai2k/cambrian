# Helicoprion — authored Tripo body and measured procedural twin, one shared rig

**Status: built, measured and rendered; not shipped.** `helicoprion` is deliberately *not* in
`tools/triassic/shipped.json` and its preview badge in
`src/content/triassic/pending-refinements.json` is untouched, so nothing in the game or the viewer
has changed. The animal still borrows its Devonian stand-in in play. This directory is the
candidate and the evidence for it; a human decides whether it ships.

The delivered pair keeps the generated body's fusiform trunk, its five median fins, its paired
pectorals and pelvics and its lunate tail, on one 23-joint skeleton with the same three sockets and
**21 byte-for-byte identical decoded animation performances**. The twin is also the runtime LOD.

| Delivery | Triangles | Vertices | Packaged bytes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `helicoprion.glb` — authored Tripo body, the generation's own whorl kept and filled | 19,253 | 11,619 | 1,501,544 |
| `helicoprion.puppet.glb` — procedural twin | 7,100 | 3,548 | 589,028 |
| `helicoprion.lod1.glb` — byte-identical twin alias | 7,100 | 3,548 | 589,028 |

The three GLBs and `helicoprion.json` are in `public/assets/triassic/creatures/`. The portraits are
**not**: they are in [`portraits/`](portraits/) here, because the roster's placeholder card, select
and thumbnail cut from the canonical pose are what the game currently draws, and overwriting them
would change the game for an animal that has not been passed. Shipping means copying those four
files across, adding the id to `shipped.json`, running `node tools/update-asset-sizes.mjs`, and
adding a creature row to `src/content/triassic/specimens.json` so the viewer's *Body* switch can
show the pair.

The model is 5.000 engine authoring units long, faces +Z in glTF and uses +Y up. The research
registry (`docs/research/triassic-swimming.json`) gives the animal 5 m; its movement and size
fields are generated by `npm run triassic:stats` and nothing here touches them.

## The tooth whorl — read this before anything else

The whorl is the one feature this subject was greenlit on (`requiredFeature: "single lower-jaw
tooth whorl"` in `docs/triassic/canonical/manifest.json`), and it has now been through three
states. The first delivery kept the generation's own whorl. A reviewer looking at the shipped body
said *"it should read a bit more like a spiral saw, whereas right now it feels like it has holes
and inconsistent shape"*, and the answer to that was a **procedurally authored logarithmic coil**
of 149 crowns. The reviewer looked at that and asked for the other thing instead: *"Don't recreate
the whorl, just see if you can 'fill it in'."*

**So the coil is reverted and the generation's whorl is back.** What this builder does to it is
only what "fill it in" can mean without inventing shape: it shuts the whorl's own narrow gaps by
moving its own two walls together. Nothing is added, nothing is re-spaced, no UV moves, and the
crowns are not touched.

### What was there to fill, measured

The number the complaint had been turned into was the audit's **radial gap**: walking outward from
a fitted centre along each of 72 rays, how far is it from one piece of whorl material to the next?
On the delivered body 43 of 65 rays crossed a gap wider than 1 % of the body, worst 0.0490, mean
0.0154.

That number is mostly a statement about **vertex density**. The whorl is described by 382
vertices, under three per crown for an animal that carried about 130, so the rays are largely
measuring the spaces between vertices rather than spaces in the surface. Asked of the same faces
sampled over their *area*, the same measure gives:

| Helicoprion's whorl, measured about one fitted centre | rays over 1 % | mean gap | worst |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| over its vertices, as the first audit asked | 56 of 72 | 0.0154 | 0.0335 |
| over its **surface**, before this builder touches it | **24 of 72** | **0.0081** | 0.0256 |
| over its surface, after the fill | 24 of 72 | 0.0081 | 0.0265 |

Restricted to the symphyseal coil and measured on the raw generation the same way, the surface
figure falls from 43-of-65 to **9 of 72**, and the three worst of those nine sit in one 15°
sector at the coil's lower rear — which is not a hole in the coil but the place where the coil
ends and the mouth begins. Everywhere else the surface is continuous.

**The surface is solid.** `fill_whorl` finds only **11 vertices** in the whole coil looking across
a gap narrower than `WHORL_SLOT` (0.0035) at another piece of whorl facing back at them; closing
those moves 164 vertices by at most **0.0017** of a body length, and the radial-gap measure does
not move, because what it is still reporting is not holes.

The three rows above are the builder's own before/after, taken about **one** fitted centre, which
is the only way the two are comparable. `whorl-audit.json` fits its centre separately on the source
and on the built body, from vertex sets that differ (382 against 369, because the built body is
classified at 5× and rounds differently), so its `source` and `built` blocks are each internally
honest and should not be read as a difference.

### What the "holes" actually are

They are the **albedo**. [`whorl-albedo.jpg`](whorl-albedo.jpg) is the same body at the same three
gapes from the same two cameras, drawn once with the generated 2K Tripo texture and once with a
single flat material. Under the texture the coil is a bloodied, mottled mass with dark ragged
patches that read as voids. Under one flat material the same geometry is a legible spiral saw: a
coil of even triangular crowns spiralling outward from a closed centre, with a serrated rim.

So the honest answer to *"fill it in"* is that there is very little geometry to fill, and that
filling it does not change what the reviewer is looking at. **The remaining decision is about the
texture, not the mesh**, and it is a decision rather than a fix, so it has not been taken here:

- the mesh could keep the generated albedo, which is what ships now;
- or the whorl's own faces could be re-seated onto a clean pale patch of **this animal's own**
  albedo — enamel rather than gum — which is a UV change and no new texture. A trial of exactly
  that was rendered during this work and it does read as a spiral saw. It was not shipped because
  the region's boundary is a hard edge with no way to feather it: the coil came out crisply white
  against pink skin with a jagged margin at the faces where the region stops, and half a repaint
  is worse than none;
- or the pose and the generation go round again, which is the pipeline's own answer to a shape or
  a colour that is wrong.

### Placement was not re-opened, and is not in question

A proportion audit had concluded that the Tripo mesh models the whorl as a toothed disc hanging
outside and below the chin — the "pizza cutter" reconstruction Tapanila & Pruitt 2013 overturned.
`whorl-audit.py` measured it instead of taking the verdict, and the finding was **corrected** in
`docs/triassic/proportion-audit.md` §8: **374 of 382 whorl vertices (97.9 %) have chin skin
directly beneath them**, so the generated whorl is inside the mouth, and the 8 that do not are the
exposed front arc the reconstruction calls for. Nothing here moves it.

### The coil that was built and rejected

[`whorl-coil-rejected.jpg`](whorl-coil-rejected.jpg) is the record: the generation's whorl above,
the authored coil below, at Idle, Bite and Attack from the side and from three-quarter. The coil
was one logarithmic spiral of 149 crowns, 22 per volution at a constant 16.36°, expanding 1.32 per
volution on a filled plate, and measured against the same rays it did close the gaps — worst 0.0167
against 0.0490, rays over 1 % from 43 of 65 to 9 of 69. It is in git history (`7075bc9`) and can be
restored. What it lost is the thing the reviewer kept it for: it reads as a machined part, a flat
pale gear in an organic mouth, and no measurement was going to say so.

The same commit also smoothed away the dentition the animal never had — no upper teeth at all, none
on the mandible outside the symphysis (Tapanila & Pruitt 2013) — and that went back with the coil,
because the sweep's zone covers the coil's own seat and would have taken the generated whorl with
it. **So the generated upper-jaw teeth and lip rows are present again**, and they are a real defect
in the generation: `whorl-audit.json` measures the upper jaw's maximum protrusion at 0.0008 of a
body length with no vertex over the 0.0018 threshold, so they are low, but they are there in the
renders as a serrated margin along both jaws. Removing them without touching the whorl means a
sweep that excludes the coil's own volume, which is a separate piece of work.

### A voxel closing was tried, and it eats the saw

The other way to fill a structure like this is a localised voxel closing — dilate, remesh, erode,
remesh — which bridges any gap under the closing radius using only the geometry that is already
there. It was built and rendered on this whorl at radii from 0.0015 to 0.008. At the radius that
closes anything it also **blunts every crown into a soft lump**: the serrated rim goes, the coil
becomes a knobbly disc, and the result is both less legible than the generation and 6,000
triangles heavier, with its UVs to re-project. That is the brief's own warning about "a smooth
disc rather than a saw", and it is why the fill here is a displacement on the original topology
rather than a remesh.

### The mouth still cannot close

Unchanged, and for the same reason as the first delivery: in the bind pose the whorl is at
occlusion, so a clip that closed past it would drive it up through the roof of the mouth.
`audit.mjs` asserts `minRadians > −0.005` on all 21 clips. **It still only opens.**

Pelvic and anal fins are present in the mesh **and in the canonical pose**, where the design's body
plan (*Fadenia*-shaped: "fusiform, a lunate tail, no pelvic fins") says there should be none. That
one is in the pose, so it is a redraw-and-regreenlight question, not a mesh question.

## Source and intake

The preserved source is `tripo-raw/helicoprion.raw.glb`, SHA-256
`f1a5749617f8c67c7b25edbbff6d14c0d0d0c7ee98536d8e1d2a5c61cb7dc6a1`, with its 2048² albedo at
`b0663a54163d362675e829a950f09a0f09d75c376af77635d20af21575c81b30`. It is never changed.

**Orientation was established from the geometry, not taken from the estimate.**
`tools/triassic/preview-orientation.json` guesses yaw 0 for this body and says in its own comment
that it is a previewing estimate. Measuring the raw mesh: the long axis is Blender Y (1.000 against
0.490 and 0.432), the snout is the narrow end at y −0.500 (14 vertices in the terminal slice), the
first dorsal fin peaks at z +0.216 and the pectorals hang to z −0.216, so head is −Y and up is +Z.
`tx()` scales by 5 and nothing else; `export_yup` then puts the head at glTF **+Z** and up at **+Y**,
where every shipped body in this repository keeps them. The estimate happened to be right.

Intake welds the texture-seam split vertices (11,602 → 9,615) and finds **one** closed component
with no detached flakes, so nothing is deleted. 19,253 triangles survive. Eyes are modelled in the
generated surface and are already seated; no eye surgery was needed.

The material follows the correction the era's first two bodies established: the full original UV
albedo is retained, `COLOR_0` is set white so runtime recolouring does not multiply the texture by
a baked copy of its own pigment, the generated tangent-space normal map is cut from strength 1 to
**0.15**, and the linked ORM roughness/metallic inputs are disconnected before roughness is set to
**0.62** (a shade glossier than the nothosaur's 0.7 — wet cartilaginous skin) and metallic to 0.

## The jaw is skinned, not cut

Nothosaurus and Shonisaurus both arrived as closed shells with no oral cavity, so a rigid lower jaw
had to be cut free along the mouth seam and an interior modelled behind it, or a membrane would
stretch across the gape. **This generation is different and the recipe was not copied.** It already
carries a modelled open mouth — palate, an oral floor and a throat — so there is no membrane to
cut. The generated whorl's front arc *touched* the palate at the midline, at 0.0002 raw units of
clearance, and it does again now that the generated whorl is back, so no separating surface exists
that does not slice one of them. That is why the jaw is a skinned hinge and why the mouth only
ever opens.

The jaw is therefore a **skinned hinge**. A measured separation surface runs down the head between
the palate above and the whorl/mouth floor below — thirteen stations from z −0.030 at the snout
through −0.003 at frac 0.20 of the head to −0.050 at the throat, dropping a further 0.40 per unit
of half-width because the palate is arched — and the weight is a smoothstep across it with a 0.006
band, faded out behind the hinge over y −0.355 → −0.305. The shell stays watertight, which a cut
here would not, and there is no hole at the hinge to fill with hinge tissue.

The consequence, and it is a real constraint: the jaw can only ever **open**. In the bind pose the
whorl is already at occlusion, so a clip that closed past it would drive the tooth spiral up
through the roof of the mouth. `audit.mjs` asserts that on all 21 clips, measuring the jaw bone's
own quaternion through the packed GLB: `minRadians > −0.005` everywhere.

## Rig

23 joints: `root`, `body`, `chest`, `skull`, `jaw`; `tail_00…tail_06`; `caudal_upper` and
`caudal_lower`; `dorsal`; and `pec_upper/pec_mid/pec_tip` and `pelvic` per side.

The axial chain blends longitudinally between ten stations. The second dorsal and the anal fin have
**no bones of their own** and ride the caudal chain, which is what a shark's rigid median fins do;
the first dorsal gets one because it is large enough for a slight lag to read.

Fins are separated from the flank they grow out of by **measured shell thickness**, not by a
guessed boundary: a ray cast inward along each vertex's normal gives the local thickness, which is
0.005–0.015 of a body length on every blade and 0.18 on the trunk. That single measurement was not
enough on its own, and the failure is worth recording: a vertex right on a fin's *rim* has a normal
lying almost in the plane of the blade, so its ray runs the length of the fin instead of across it
and the rim measured as thick as the trunk. The first build weighted the rim of the left pectoral
to `body`/`chest` while the blade around it was weighted to `pec_tip_L`, and the Death roll tore a
fan of spikes out of the fin (one vertex travelled 2.96 units against 2.60 for the blade it belongs
to). Taking the **minimum thickness over a two-ring neighbourhood** fixes it: every outlier in the
Death pose is now a clean `pec_tip_L: 1.0`, and the largest displacement is the fin tip's own
2.602 units. Fin roots blend radially from inside the trunk outward, so a seated root follows the
flank when the body bends, and both pectoral and pelvic roots are asserted at build time to lie
inside the trunk's own cross-section.

Every vertex on both bodies has normalised non-zero weights and at most **four** influences.

## The twin

A **voxel volume resurfacing** at 0.0052 raw units, relaxed and reduced. No source vertex or face
survives it. Pigment is sampled through the nearest source triangle's own interpolated UV with a
bilinear texture lookup, so nothing averages unrelated atlas islands at a welded seam.

Two things about this body needed handling that Nothosaurus's twin did not, and both are in the
builder with the numbers that forced them:

- **Relaxation eats fins.** Two unmasked passes of smoothing on a blade two voxels thick simply
  remove it. The first build lost the anal fin outright — 0.317 units of envelope error at station
  +1.225 against a 0.2 tolerance. The relaxation is now masked by the twin's own measured shell
  thickness, so it takes the staircase off the trunk and never touches a blade.
- **A voxel field cannot hold a knife edge.** Every fin here tapers to nothing, and an occupancy
  field sampled at 0.0052 stops about two and a half voxels short of the trailing tip; the station
  at +1.225 fell straight into the gap the anal fin's missing 0.014 left. The blades — and only the
  blades, by their own measured thickness — are therefore dilated 0.005 along their normals *on the
  twin's copy* before the field is sampled, which carries the rim out as well as thickening the
  plate. The twin's fins come back about five thousandths of a body plumper than the authored ones
  and reach the same tips. **The authored body is never touched by this.**

`helicoprion-profile.json` records **21 exact plane-intersection envelopes** of both actual meshes.

| nearest-twin-surface distance over authored vertices, max | 0.12055 | 2.41 % | — |
| the same, 95th percentile | 0.03129 | 0.63 % | — |
| `anchor_mouth` to the nearest authored surface | 0.00533 | **0.11 %** | 0.1 (2 %) |
| `anchor_mouth_inside` to the nearest authored surface | 0.06074 | **1.21 %** | 0.1 (2 %) |
| `anchor_attack_primary` to the nearest authored surface | 0.01899 | **0.38 %** | 0.1 (2 %) |

The envelope is 0.78 % of body length against the 4 % the pipeline allows, and the surface distance
is back where the first delivery had it — **1.70 %** at the maximum and **0.57 %** at the 95th
percentile, against the 2.41 % and 0.63 % the authored coil cost. `anchor_mouth_inside` is back at
its constant (0, −0.362, −0.024) and so lands 1.21 % of a body length off the nearest surface,
which is inside tolerance but is mid-cavity rather than on the throat floor: with the generated
dentition present again there is material there, which is what the constant was chosen against.

The jaw hinge is a joint rather than a surface feature, so it is checked the other way: it is one
shared bone, so its parity error between the two bodies is zero by construction, and `audit.mjs`
asserts the whole skeleton, every inverse-bind matrix and every socket transform equal between the
authored body, the twin and the LOD.

These are generated measurements. They are not a claimed human anatomical sign-off, and no
reviewer is invented by this record.

## Motion

The full 21-clip set the era's fullest body carries: Idle, Swim, Sprint, TurnLeft, TurnRight, Dive,
Rise, Attack, Bite, Heavy, Hit, Death, Guard, Parry, Dodge, Eat, Stagger, Ability, Grab, Breath,
Growth.

| Clip | s | | Clip | s | | Clip | s |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| Idle\* | 2.4 | | Attack | 1.0 | | Dodge | 0.5 |
| Swim\* | 1.6 | | Bite | 0.5 | | Eat\* | 1.6 |
| Sprint\* | 1.0 | | Heavy | 1.1 | | Stagger | 1.2 |
| TurnLeft | 1.6 | | Hit | 0.6 | | Ability | 1.0 |
| TurnRight | 1.6 | | Death | 1.6 | | **Grab\*** | **1.1** |
| Dive | 1.4 | | Guard\* | 1.0 | | Breath | 2.4 |
| Rise | 1.4 | | Parry | 0.4 | | Growth | 1.5 |

`*` loops exactly; the loop seam is 0.0 to the float on all six. Grab is a held loop at 1.1 s, in
the 0.9–1.2 s the contract asks for, and it loops rather than playing once because the renderer
plays it with `playLoop` while a rider is attached. Root motion and scale animation are absent.

**Locomotion is body-caudal undulation and nothing else.** This animal has no limbs to row or walk
with, no haul-out and no air, so nothing in the performance does any of those. One travelling wave
runs down the axial chain with the amplitude growing backwards and a phase lag per joint, and the
paired fins are control surfaces: they set pitch and roll, they bank the turns and brace the
guard, and they never take a stroke. Measured off the packed GLB played through Three.js at 121
phases (`paired-audit.json`, `gait`):

| | Swim | Sprint |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| skull lateral travel | 0.0128 | 0.0200 |
| `tail_00` | 0.0142 | 0.0220 |
| `tail_03` | 0.1831 | 0.2810 |
| `tail_06` | 0.6336 | 0.8987 |
| caudal lobe tip | 1.3666 | 1.7374 |
| caudal lobe lag behind the peduncle, fraction of a beat | 0.038 | 0.040 |

The head is held on the line of travel deliberately. `body` is the pivot of the wave, so its own
sway has to be taken back out by the two bones in front of it or the snout swings further than the
tail does; the chest counter-yaws by the ratio of the two lever arms (body→skull 1.375 units
against chest→skull 0.5) and the snout keeps a little sway of its own. Without that the skull
travelled further than the first caudal joint, which is a fish swimming backwards.

Clip by clip: turns bank hard and fly the bank on the pectorals (inside fin down, outside fin up)
rather than steering with the tail alone; Dive and Rise pitch the trunk and angle both pectorals;
Attack and Heavy have anticipation, gape, strike, recoil and recovery; Bite is a short gape and
snap; Ability is the roster's **whorl saw** — a held gape with the body rolling and dragging back
three times over the clip, which is the "grip that shortens the target" the creature card
describes — and Grab is the same clamp held as a loop with a slower tug. Death rolls the animal
2.55 rad belly-up and sinks it. Breath is **gill ventilation held in place**, not a surface breath:
this animal has gills, never goes up, and the clip exists only so the body carries the same set as
the era's fullest one. Locomotor travel and the grip rules stay engine-owned.

## Verification

`node tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/audit.mjs --package --decode` meshopt-compresses the
three files, asserts that packing changed no animation sample and no mesh attribute, and then
asserts, for the authored body, the twin and the LOD alike:

- 21 clips by name, 23 joints, 3 sockets with `cambrianAnchor` version 1 metadata;
- **exact** paired joint names, hierarchy, local rest transforms, inverse-bind arrays, socket
  transforms and metadata, and per-clip SHA-256 of every sample array;
- no duplicate clips, no root channel, no scale channel, every value finite, loop seams under 1e-4
  on all six looping clips, and visible motion on all 21;
- normalised skin weights on every vertex; the twin under 40 % of the authored triangles
  (**28.27 %**, the authored body having grown by the whorl); `lod1` byte-identical to the twin.

Then the Three.js `GLTFLoader`/`AnimationMixer` plays **61 phases of every clip on both models**,
evaluating sampled actual skinned vertices, and the species checks run on the played rig: the beat
grows backwards, the head is the quiet end, the caudal lobe lags the peduncle, and the jaw opens
without ever closing past bind. Measurements are written to `paired-audit.json` *before* they are
judged, so a failure leaves its numbers behind. The Blender build additionally checks every vertex
of both bodies at 13 phases per clip for finiteness and bounds.

The build is reproducible, and was re-checked after the whorl was generated rather than inherited:
run end to end a second time, `build.py` followed by `audit.mjs --package --decode` produced the
three GLBs **byte for byte identical** (sha256 unchanged on all three), and the envelope, anchor
and whorl figures repeated to the last digit.

Repository checks run green with these files present: `npm run triassic` (582 checks),
`npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm run props`, `npm run eras`.
`node tools/update-asset-sizes.mjs` was run and is a no-op, as it should be: it records only ids
listed in `shipped.json`, and this one deliberately is not.

## Honest limitations

1. **The fill barely fills anything, and that is the finding.** Only 11 vertices in the whole coil
   look across a gap narrow enough to call a slot; closing them moves 164 vertices by at most
   0.0017 of a body length and does not move the radial-gap measure at all. If the reviewer's word
   "holes" meant the mesh, there was almost nothing there to close; the measure that said otherwise
   was counting gaps between vertices.
1b. **What reads as holes is the generated albedo**, which this build does not touch. See
   `whorl-albedo.jpg` and the section above: the same geometry under one flat material is a clean
   spiral saw. A UV re-seat onto the animal's own pale skin was tried and not shipped, because the
   region boundary cannot be feathered and the result was patchy. That decision is open.
1c. **The dentition the animal never had is back.** Reverting the coil reverted the sweep that took
   the generation's upper-jaw teeth, mandibular teeth and the row under the chin with it, because
   that sweep's zone covers the whorl's own seat. The teeth are low — maximum protrusion 0.0008 of
   a body length, nothing over the 0.0018 threshold — but they are visible in the renders as a
   serrated margin along both jaws, and *Helicoprion* had none of them. Removing them without
   touching the whorl needs a sweep that excludes the coil's own volume.
2. **Pelvic and anal fins** are on an animal the design says had none — but they are in the greenlit
   pose, so that is a pose question.
3. **The caudal lobe lag is slight** (0.038–0.040 of a beat). A lunate tail's fin should trail its
   peduncle more visibly. The lobe bones are there and the number is easy to raise; I left it where
   the motion still looked controlled rather than floppy.
4. **The twin's fins are about 0.005 of a body plumper** than the authored ones by construction.
   That is inside every tolerance but it is a real difference, and at the LOD distances the twin is
   drawn at it will read as very slightly thicker fins.
4b. **The twin has no whorl.** A 0.0052 voxel cannot resolve denticles 0.005 across, so the twin's
   mouth is a bulge. Good enough for a distant model; if the twin is ever shown close the head
   wants resurfacing at a finer voxel than the body.
5. **Surface distance max 1.70 %**, 95th percentile 0.57 % — back to the first delivery's figures,
   because the body is the first delivery's geometry with 164 vertices nudged.
5b. **The skin tears at the pectoral roots, and always has.**
   `node tools/triassic/skin-tears.mjs public/assets/triassic/creatures/helicoprion.glb` measures
   every edge against its rest length over all 21 clips: the worst is **11.68x on `pec_upper_R`**
   in Parry, with 263 edges past 2x, and 17 of 21 clips tear something. The bones named are
   `chest`, `pec_upper_L/R` — the paired-fin roots — so this is the fin weighting, not the whorl,
   and it is not something this work introduced. For scale, the era's cleanest body (Nothosaurus)
   is 2.98x. It wants a pass over the pectoral weights before this animal ships.
5c. **Reverting the coil halved the tearing.** The same tool on the coil body measured **23.10x on
   `skull`** with 991 torn edges in Bite alone, all of them skull and jaw: the coil rode the `jaw`
   bone at weight 1 while the palate it touched rode `skull`, so every edge across that contact was
   pulled the whole width of the gape. Restoring the generated whorl takes Bite from 22.05x/991 to
   4.20x/179 and the worst clip from 23.10x to 11.68x. Nothing in the paired audits could see it.
6. **The bind pose has an open mouth** and cannot be given a closed one, because the whorl is
   already at occlusion. Every clip opens from there.
7. **No material-comparison study** of the kind Nothosaurus needed was done, because no crumpled
   or streaked appearance was observed here; the same corrections were applied preventively. If the
   body is inspected in the live viewer and looks wrong, that study has not been done and would be
   the next step.
8. **No `sculpt:measure` cross-check.** The profile comparison here is the builder's own 21-station
   plane-intersection measurement, which is the pipeline's stated check for step 6, but the viewer's
   sculpt measurement was not additionally run against it.
9. **Portraits are not installed**, by choice — see the top of this file.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, with Blender 5.2 (`npm run blender`) and the project's Node dependencies:

```sh
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/build.py
node tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/audit.mjs --package --decode
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/render.py -- --decoded
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/render.py -- --decoded --twin
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/render.py -- --decoded --whorl-only
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/render.py -- --decoded --whorl-only --flat
python3 tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/contact-sheets.py
/opt/blender/blender --background --factory-startup --python tools/triassic/creatures/helicoprion/whorl-audit.py
node tools/triassic/review-bodies.mjs
```

`whorl-audit.py` reads the decoded body `audit.mjs --decode` writes, so run it after the audit and
not before. `--whorl-only` is the pair of close-ups the whorl work is judged on: the coil filling
the frame from the side, which is the plane it lies in, and from three-quarter looking in past the
snout, which is how a player meets it. `--whorl-only --flat` is the same six frames with the body
on one matte material instead of its albedo, and `contact-sheets.py` puts the two rows together as
`whorl-albedo.jpg`.

`build.py` authors both geometry and performance and writes only this species' asset family. It
modifies no shared registry and performs no git operations. Headless Blender has no GL context, so
every render here is Cycles on the CPU; the Workbench engine needs EGL and will fail. The paired
editable Blender project, the decoded review GLBs and the individual frames live in
`local/triassic-authoring/helicoprion/`, which is not committed.
