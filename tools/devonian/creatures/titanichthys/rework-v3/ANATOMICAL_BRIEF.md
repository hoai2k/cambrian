# Titanichthys V3 — new sculpt brief

The intended animal is a substantial, broad-headed armoured swimmer. Its mass comes from a deep cheek and oral chamber suspended beneath a broad cranial roof, a rounded ventral throat, tall thoracic armour, and a tapering muscular posterior. The reference's three-dimensional anterior body is the main artistic direction. This is a new construction, with no old geometry or material imports.

## Evidence and the reference

Boyle & Ryan's generic diagnosis describes a broad, dorsoventrally compressed head shield, a shield length/width ratio of 0.5–0.54, thin plates, slender toothless lower jaws with downturned anterior tips, and paired lateral shield fenestrae. Their reconstruction concerns the armour and does not establish the full soft-tissue head's depth. The clay therefore keeps a wide, relatively shallow roof above substantial cheeks and throat. Its cranial roof design envelope is 1.50 long by 2.92 wide, ratio 0.514; this is a design landmark, not a claim that the whole head is fossil-measured. Shallow covered lateral depressions suggest the fenestra regions without inventing open holes through living skin. The exact plate map is interpretive and is not a specimen tracing. [Boyle & Ryan (2017), *New information on Titanichthys…*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-paleontology/article/new-information-on-titanichthys-placodermi-arthrodira-from-the-cleveland-shale-upper-devonian-of-ohio-usa/F9C6D35916E95652B86894AAF33D8DDF)

Coatham and colleagues describe the 96 cm inferognathal of *T. termieri*, small relative eyes, and edentulous jaw morphology. Their mechanical comparison supports suspension feeding; preserved filter anatomy is absent. Thus the lower margin must remain narrow, smooth and without crushing plates, tusks, teeth, or baleen. Large gape and substantial soft oral walls can coexist with a slender bony jaw. Clay folds are soft-tissue interpretation. [Coatham et al. (2020), *Was the Devonian placoderm Titanichthys a suspension feeder?*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7277245/)

The supplied image is private appearance reference, not anatomical evidence. Use its body depth, muscular head-to-shoulder transition and long fin sweep. Reject its cutting-looking jaw projections. Whole-body proportions, fins, heterocercal tail, soft tissues, motion, colour and fine surface treatment remain artistic hypotheses. Colour work follows approval of shape; this pass uses neutral clay so pigment cannot disguise weak volumes.

## Shape construction and visual priorities

1. **Head:** broad arched roof, domed interorbital volume, modest brow and recessed small spherical eyes. Flattened plate planes wrap onto the cheeks. The cheek flare, lower cheek hollow, hinge region and ventral throat are distinct sculpted forms in one outer skin, not attached shield tiles or balls.
2. **Jaw and cavity:** a wide, softly rounded aperture leads through a rolled lip into palate, cheeks, floor and a rearward/downturned throat. Narrow lip rails are sewn into the continuous cavity boundary. The downturned lower-front tissue covers a slender inferred inferognathal; no blades. A separate wide-gape shape study rotates the anterior lower jaw around the lateral hinge and lifts the cranial roof slightly, with a continuous commissure transition.
3. **Thorax:** the dorsal armour rises behind the roof, while broad lateral plates enclose the body depth. A restrained nuchal articulation groove separates cranial and thoracic regions. Fine grooves are incised into the continuous skin; plate crowns and planes do the principal visual work. The lateral/ventral outline must not remain a flat shield.
4. **Posterior:** the widest body gives way to a shaped belly, epaxial ridge and asymmetrical upper/lower contours. Myomere-scale shallow relief belongs only to the flexible region. The caudal peduncle continues into a fleshy upper-lobe support, with a broad lower membrane and distinct fork.
5. **Fins:** long swept pectorals emerge from broad buried fleshy bases, then narrow into cambered membranes and rounded tips. The trailing edge has a relaxed downward roll, not a straight slab. Pelvic fins remain subordinate; the modest rear dorsal and tail are coherent reconstructions. Fin rays appear as slight membrane relief, not free needles.

Coordinates use X lateral, negative Y forward, Z up. Total clay length is about 7.4 construction units and maximum pectoral span about 6.7 units; no biological size conclusion follows from these units. The specimen remains identified as the *T. termieri*-informed reconstruction, while genus-level roof traits are explicitly comparative.

## Clay review acceptance

- Side: deep cranial/cheek block and thorax occupy real height; roof, cheek, throat and posterior read as different connected structures. No barrel with a pasted-on shield.
- Front: broad head, rounded ventral jaw, tall cheek walls, small lateral eyes, open lined mouth, bilateral fin roots. No flat skate/disc silhouette.
- Dorsal: broad short roof retained; long fins taper gracefully; posterior becomes a muscular peduncle rather than a skinny stick.
- Obliques: plate planes and recessed seams belong to the continuous form. Root-to-fin transitions are broad and concealed inside body tissue. Eye exposure is modest with no separate eyewear-like rims.
- Oral and gape: broad smooth edentulous margins; interior does not expose the outside skin, fin roots or a bright end plug. Cheeks remain connected in the gape study.
- New candidate only: inspect these exact renders before any rig/material finalization. Numerical topology checks in the builder establish basic construction integrity but cannot approve art. No old V2 audit is transferred.

## Rig, action and material direction after clay approval

Keep cranial and thoracic armour rigid on separate skull/body controls with a limited neck articulation. A jaw hinge behind the mouth drives depression; oral floor and commissures blend between jaw, skull and throat. Never stretch the broad shield to simulate gape. The posterior needs at least four phase-delayed bending controls, a separate caudal trim control and soft transitions out of the rigid thorax. Each pectoral needs base, mid and distal controls to combine banking support with subtle curvature; pelvic and dorsal motion stays quieter. Suspension-feeding actions should use slow sustained gape, throat ventilation and restrained fin trim, while contact motions should lead with the shield. Preserve established clip names only after new actions are authored and judged individually.

Future pigment: related dorsal slate/olive hues, warmer subdued belly, low-contrast armour mottling and finer posterior texture. No colour is asserted as fossil evidence. Final sources, GLBs, LODs, sockets, eyes, attachments and all actions require a new hash-bound review after the completed rework.
