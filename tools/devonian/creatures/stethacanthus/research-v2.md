# Stethacanthus V2 reconstruction decisions

The Devonian occurrence is CMNH 8988, a spine-brush complex with associated teeth from the upper Famennian Cleveland Shale. Ginter & Sun (2007, p.710) question Williams' assignment to S. altonensis; this asset is therefore **Stethacanthus sp.**, not a reconstruction labelled Akmonistion zangerli. Its body is explicitly comparative, not a complete specimen scan.

- Short skull with a terminal mouth, comparatively large eyes embedded into the actual continuous cranial tissue; no projecting orbital hoops.
- Relatively slender five-cusped grasping teeth with substantial lateral cusps. Do not use the exaggerated broad central cusp of the later true S. altonensis comparison.
- Spine-supported first dorsal brush: tissue-covered supported structure, forward directed enlarged dermal denticles on crown, opposing backwards directed cranial field. Irregular gradation, subtle bilateral asymmetry and small replacement-sized elements; not identical spike rows or external hair.
- No brush inflation: Coates et al. (1998) reject the earlier erectile interpretation because the comparative brush has a mineralized keel, rods and baseplate. Their Bearsden anatomy is a structural comparison, not a Devonian body substitution. A male complex-bearing configuration is a comparative interpretation and does not determine CMNH 8988's sex.
- Smoothly attached paired fins, long metapterygial axes interpreted as narrow posterior extensions, small spineless rear dorsal, epicercal caudal axis but comparatively balanced external fin outline.
- Recessed branchial clefts rather than raised cords. Continuous shaped mouth with palate, floor, cheeks and curved pharynx; articulated lower jaw with continuous skin/lining weights.
- Pigmentation, precise fin-whip length, living body proportions, oral mucosa and action timing remain reconstructed. No modern shark slicing teeth, caudal keels, exaggerated Akmonistion body plan or moving brush denticles are asserted.
