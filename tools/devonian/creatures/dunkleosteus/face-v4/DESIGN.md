# Dunkleosteus face V4 — candidate01 sculpt study

This is a focused local design candidate, not an approved model or a replacement public family.

The new user reference has a much stronger face than the V2 source: the heavy brow shelters the eye, a large angular cheek dominates the side plane, and conspicuous cutting structures grow from substantial roots. In V2's actual final-review images, the head reads as a smooth tapered wedge, the eyes as exposed buttons and the oral cutting surfaces as thin flat strips. The accepted posterior body and material treatment already work and are preserved.

The study reshapes the existing closed head envelope. A broad cheek plateau with a narrow sloping rim and recessed transition creates physical facial mass; an integrated brow ridge adds orbital support above the unchanged globes; a raised blunt front makes the skull silhouette less smoothly conical. These are authored interpretive dimensions, not measured fossil landmarks. The slight relief is deliberately continuous with the head and is not a set of floating armour plates. The original calm cranial pigmentation and all packed texture bytes remain unchanged. Old painted sutures may need later alignment if they fight the new contour; judge this in actual renders before changing materials.

Each side retains an anterior supragnathal, posterior supragnathal and inferognathal. New cross sections have wide roots, convex shoulders, a thin chisel edge, shaped anterior points and a continuous shearing region. The upper posterior root now sits above its cutting edge. The lower anterior cusp is emphasized but the rear is one long blade, not a row of ordinary conical teeth. Fine fang-like points and root curvature follow the user's visual request; their exact form and exposure remain art interpretation. Do not reproduce the reference's numerous accessory cusps as though each were fossil-supported.

Primary anatomical context: [Engelman 2024, Head and Mouthparts](https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/2024/5307-dunkleosteus-reconstruction). It describes long cheek/jaw units, terminal gnathal projection, blunt snout and large orbits; skin coverage and lips remain open questions. Its submarginal discussion also cautions against presenting a single decorative cheek outline as an exact suture map. These constrain the study, which is original game sculpture rather than a fossil reproduction. A gnathal histology paper was identified (Lebedev et al. 2023, DOI 10.3390/d15050648), but the publisher returned HTTP 429; no claim in this brief relies on unread contents.

The independently moving head/jaw, lower-jaw envelope, oral floor/palate/cheek lining/pharynx, all 18 action curves, all other meshes, eye globes and three anchors are frozen from the saved V2. The script checks exact unchanged mesh/UV/colour/weight hashes, bone geometry, action keys, anchor transforms and packed image bytes before saving the candidate. Those preservation checks do not prove contact clearance or eye embedding after sculpting.

Inspect rest side/front/oblique; Heavy max gape front/oblique; Bite mid action side; Eat; and Heavy recovery. Primary questions are whether the new lower cusp actually receives the upper gnathals in closure, whether thick roots have coherent support in the oral shell, whether cheek relief is visible at specimen scale, and whether the brow adequately frames the unchanged globe without becoming a second eyelid band. Reject any exposed seam, swallowed cusp, abrupt intersection or smooth toy-like transition.
