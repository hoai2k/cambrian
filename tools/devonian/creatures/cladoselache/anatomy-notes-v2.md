# Cladoselache V2 anatomical decisions

This is an independently modeled genus-level Cleveland Shale reconstruction, not a specimen scan. Soft-tissue volume, pigment, eye appearance, movement timing and detailed oral folds are artistic inference. The characteristic fossil constraints take precedence over a modern shark template.

## Sources consulted

Its exceptionally wide specialized nasal architecture, documented posterior spine, and detailed scale distribution are not transferred wholesale.

## Reconstruction choices

A short, blunt nasal roof, substantial lateral eyes and a forward opening distinguish the head from a pointed modern lamnid shark. The lower jaw narrows anteriorly in plan. The palate, cheeks and flexible floor are continuous with the lip opening and converge gradually into a recessed pharyngeal passage. Small central cusps and smaller lateral cusplets represent cladodont grasping dentition; serrated triangular cutting teeth are excluded.

Broad pectoral fins have long, well supported roots and restrained surface relief over the internal radials. Pelvics remain smaller. Two dorsal fins, one stout curved anterior spine, a narrow keeled peduncle and a high aspect crescent tail define the axial silhouette. A posterior spine and anal fin are omitted. Precise fin camber and soft edge outlines remain reconstruction choices.

Five gill clefts per side are modeled as shallow depressions of the continuous skin and mapped crevice pigment, with local secondary deformation. They are not external cords. No raised lateral cord, orbital bezel or exposed fin rays remains. Regional countershading is an artistic choice, made with an original skin swatch and dedicated UV maps rather than an asserted fossil color.

Eye audits will use the actual exported globe volumes and the connected continuous head/body mesh, including the continuous oral lining. Any opening automatically closed by the independent audit will be reported with its clearance from each globe. No ornamental geometry is included in the head envelope.
