# Gemuendina — total rework, clay 01

The user reference is a direction for living mass and surface character: a
substantial rounded cranial front, shoulders that flow into flared pectorals,
cambered margins, and a tail rooted in the central body. The new mesh is authored
from zero. It does not import, stretch, subdivide or decorate V2 geometry.

## Evidence and limits

- Südkamp (2021), *Ikonen des Hunsrückschiefers*, pp.17–18, figures 18–19:
  large pectorals, smaller pelvics, tapering finless tail; upward mouth and dorsal
  branchial exits follow Gross. Pectorals are described as less mobile than
  those of modern ray-like sharks. The illustrated ventral specimen confirms
  trunk tubercles, weaker or absent on pectorals. Phosphatic covering limits
  surface interpretation. The near-complete dorsal specimen is 304 mm long.
  [Author-hosted PDF](https://www.bundenbach-fossilien.de/Literatur/2021_S%C3%BCdkamp_Ikonen.pdf).
  The local page images and PDF were inspected again for this rework.
- Johanson & Smith (2005), fig.13, Gemuendina specimens KGM 1983/306, /308 and
  /307: denticulated infragnathals constrain later oral detailing. The author
  upload and figure context were inspected; publisher full text was unavailable.
  [Published figure context](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Gemuendina-stuertzi-Rhenanida-A-B-KGM-1983-306-C-KGM-1983-308-D-KGM-1983-307_fig7_7820947),
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1464793104006682).
- Gross (1963) remains a citation through later authors, not a directly inspected
  source. No precise cranial plate map is claimed from it.

Living thickness, exact flexible margins, oral tissues, colours, eye pigmentation
and motion are reconstruction choices. The supplied image's flowing edges inform
the sculpt; rhenanid anatomy controls the mouth, branchial arrangement and tail.

## Frozen clay choices

- A single closed mesh carries cranial vault, thick cheek region, axial body,
  pectorals, pelvic lobes and tail. No overlapping fin sheets or head/wing seam.
- Length 6.06 authoring units, span 3.74, body envelope height 0.70. The scale
  setting maps length to approximately 0.30 m. Maximum thickness is concentrated
  around the cranial/branchial core and diminishes smoothly toward the tail.
- A wide rounded snout leads into raised orbital cheeks, a shallow central
  saddle and an extended postorbital shoulder. The eyes emerge from this true
  envelope; no detached pads or rings. Their clay globes are landmarks pending
  final material and independent containment review.
- The pectoral outline sweeps outward behind the head, stays full through its
  widest span, then contracts through a concave trailing transition. Its upper
  and lower surfaces taper to a rounded shared edge. Root fullness, shallow
  shoulder ridges and changing camber are built into the same surface.
- A smaller pelvic outline follows the pectoral notch. The tail transitions
  from a substantial root to a slender, round, slightly curved tip without a
  sting or any median/caudal fin.
- The dorsal transverse oral aperture has a raised preoral edge and taller
  posterior wall. Welded rings descend into an actual bent bowl and internal
  throat endpoint. The clay uses a quieter dark material inside to expose its
  depth. Jaw articulation, lining folds and small denticles follow clay review.
- Two shallow dorsal branchial crease landmarks indicate the region only;
  final recessed tissue construction follows the accepted cranial shape.

## Acceptance before detail

Inspect all eight actual Blender renders. Front/side must show substantial head
mass and a smoothly tapering underside, without a wafer-thin disk or abrupt
thickness step. Dorsal/oblique must show organic swept lobes and distinct smaller
pelvics. The frontal apron must remain rhenanid-like, with no cephalic manta
lobes. Cranial/oral close-ups must show seated dorsal eyes and a real upward
cavity with walls rather than a pasted dark oval. Fin camber must read without
noise, texture or lighting tricks. The tail must emerge continuously and remain
round near its root. A topology pass is not visual approval.

## Planned next phase, conditional on clay approval

Use a stable root, body and skull, an anterior lower-jaw arc hinged at the
commissural region, a subordinate throat, and 5–6 sequential tail segments.
Each pectoral receives three longitudinal strips with proximal and outer-edge
controls; preserve broad rigidly carried inner regions, add modest delayed
margin waves, and avoid modern-manta whole-wing flapping. Pelvic steering is
subtle. Weight transitions follow the continuous surface, not object boundaries.
Jaw weights must extend into the welded lower aperture and its lining.

Author the required actions individually after the rig is posed and reviewed;
tail phase and asymmetric edge movement should carry swimming, turning and
banking. Feeding lifts the upward mouth; it must not hinge the entire snout as
an arthrodire blade. The oral sockets must be rebuilt from this new cavity.

Material direction: mottled olive/umber dorsal core, restrained pale olive
ventrum, varied small irregular mosaic fields with shallow granular relief,
more organised but understated cranial fields, and quieter pectoral ornament.
Pigment variation should cross the shoulder smoothly. Avoid uniform hexagonal
tiling, large stone slabs, identical fin/body tubercles, or bright artificial
orbital rings. Colour and exact tessera spacing remain artistic choices. No
ImageGen call is needed for this clay phase because the supplied reference
already establishes the desired art direction.

Freeze a finished sculpt before the new eye, oral, socket, action and full/LOD
audits. Preview status persists throughout this work.
